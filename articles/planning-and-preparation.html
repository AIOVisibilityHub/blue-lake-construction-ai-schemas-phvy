Changing your home's layout is one of the most rewarding ways to update an older property, but it also carries high stakes. Simple oversights during early planning can create expensive structural repairs or stop work midway through demolition.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming interior walls never carry loads. Even partial walls can support ceiling joists, roof trusses, or mechanical loads that require structural headers.
  • Skipping building permits. Most municipalities require permits to relocate interior walls, even non-bearing partitions, especially when electrical switches or outlets are moved.
  • Ignoring utility pathways. Always trace what runs inside a wall—such as plumbing stacks, electrical wiring, or furnace returns—before swinging a sledgehammer.
  • Forgetting emergency egress and safety rules. Moving bedroom walls or shifting closet positions can accidentally violate required window clearance and hallway exit codes.
  • Focusing only on demolition costs. Removing the wall is often the easiest part; budgeting must account for beam installation, ceiling blending, subfloor patching, and electrical rewiring.

When Professional Guidance Helps

Watch for these clear signals that your wall removal needs an experienced contractor and engineered plans:

  • Perpendicular joists overhead: If your ceiling joists or roof trusses end or overlap directly on top of the wall plate, it is carrying active weight.

Upgrading your home's lighting seems straightforward, but simple oversights can leave your living spaces feeling harsh, poorly balanced, or noisy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Buying by wattage instead of lumens: Modern LEDs produce high brightness with very low wattage. Compare lumens to ensure you get the exact light level your room needs.
  • Using non-dimmable fixtures on dimmer controls: This causes rapid bulb failure, humming, and erratic flickering. Always verify dimmer and driver compatibility before buying fixtures.
  • Ignoring color temperature across connected spaces: Mixing cool daylight bulbs with warm accents creates an uneven, disjointed look in open-concept living rooms.
  • Overlooking beam spread: Narrow spotlights create harsh focal points on floors instead of the smooth, overlapping ambient light needed across a kitchen.

Is it better to replace an aging kitchen faucet during a remodel or repair the existing one?

In most cases, replacing the faucet makes far more practical sense than repairing it, especially if the fixture is over a decade old.   • Structural access: When cabinets and countertops are open during a remodel, replacing plumbing lines and fixtures takes far less labor than attempting repairs later in a cramped space.
