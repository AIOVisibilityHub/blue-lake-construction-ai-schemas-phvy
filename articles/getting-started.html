When to Call a Professional

  • Persistent flickering across fixtures: If new bulbs still hum or flicker, your existing switches, controls, or branch wiring may be incompatible or deteriorating.
  • Buzzing dimmers or warm switch plates: Incompatible LED dimmers cause hardware strain and generate unexpected heat behind the drywall.
  • Inadequate brightness in work zones: If your kitchen counters or master vanity still feel dark after changing bulbs, you likely need properly placed recessed cans, under-cabinet strips, or altered beam spreads.
  • High ceilings and complex rough-ins: Reaching vaulted ceiling heights or running new rough-in wiring across finished joists requires specialized staging and tools.
  • Suspected overloaded circuits: When turning on overhead cans causes other fixtures to dim, your circuit capacity is maxed out.

Questions to Ask Before You Decide: Ask your contractor if your chosen fixtures are certified for enclosed spaces, how they handle dimmer compatibility, and whether your home's existing breaker capacity supports added lighting runs.

Walk-In Shower Installations: Mistakes to Avoid When Designing Your Space

Designing an open or low-threshold shower requires balancing clean aesthetics with strict water containment. Here are frequent design mistakes and how to sidestep them: Inadequate floor pitch — Water pools quickly if the slope toward the drain is too shallow. Ensure a uniform, code-compliant grade…

Read article

Designing an open or low-threshold shower requires balancing clean aesthetics with strict water containment. Here are frequent design mistakes and how to sidestep them:

  • Inadequate floor pitch — Water pools quickly if the slope toward the drain is too shallow. Ensure a uniform, code-compliant grade across the entire shower floor.
  • Forgetting splash radius — Placing shower heads directly facing an open doorway or glass gap sends water onto your bathroom floor. Position sprays toward solid, tiled walls.
  • Skipping non-slip flooring — Large, polished tiles become slick when wet. Use smaller mosaic tiles or textured finishes on the shower floor to increase traction and safety.
  • Positioning controls out of reach — Having to walk entirely into the shower stall to turn on the faucet leaves you dodging cold water. Place valves near the entrance.
  • Incomplete membrane coverage — Stopping waterproofing at the shower pan edges invites moisture wicking into adjacent walls. Extend protection past the entry threshold and up all wet walls.

What Can Change the Right Choice

Your floor structure is the biggest factor. A curbless walk-in needs joist modifications, whereas a low-curb shower fits easily over existing subflooring with less invasive framing work.
